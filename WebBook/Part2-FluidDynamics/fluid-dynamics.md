# Fluid Dynamics

Over geological time the mantle flows as a very viscous fluid. This part
builds the equations of fluid motion from the conservation laws of Part I and
then specialises them to the slow, viscous flows that matter for the Earth.

- [Navier–Stokes equation](navier-stokes.md) adds viscosity to the momentum
  balance and derives the equation of motion for an incompressible fluid.
- [Steady inviscid flows](inviscid-flows.md) treats the opposite limit,
  where viscosity is negligible: Bernoulli's equation, the velocity potential
  and the stream function.
- [Non-dimensional equations](nondimensional-equations.md) scales the
  equations to identify the dimensionless numbers that control mantle flow,
  and shows why inertia can be dropped to leave Stokes flow.
- [Applications of Stokes flow](stokes-flow-applications.md) works through
  Rayleigh–Taylor instability, post-glacial rebound, phase changes, surface
  observables, folding and gravity currents.
